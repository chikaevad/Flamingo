FLAMINGO

A long-legged wading bird in six species. Standing in water nothing else can stand in. Pink because of what it eats. Scroll. ↓

EDITORIAL EDITORIAL EDITORIAL
01 / WHAT IS IT

A flamingo is a long-legged wading bird. Family Phoenicopteridae. Six species, all of them obsessed with water nothing else can drink. Named from the Latin flamma — flame.

  • — Eats upside‑down.
  • — Sleeps on one leg.
  • — Wades through brine and ash.
  • — Turns pink from food alone.
  • — Not subtle.
“The flamingo is the chemistry of its own diet, made visible.”

02 / WHY PINK

NOT A DECORATION.
A METABOLISM.

The pink comes from the food. Beta‑carotene and canthaxanthin — carotenoid pigments concentrated in spirulina, cyanobacteria and brine shrimp — pass through the flamingo's liver, get cleaved by enzymes, and deposit directly into the follicles of growing feathers, into the keratin of the beak, into the skin of the legs.

No carotenoids in the diet, no pink. Captive flamingos fed only chicken feed go white. The pigment is on loan from the lake.

03 / ANATOMY
A. BEAK INVERTED

The beak hooks downward because the bird feeds with its head upside-down, dragging the upper mandible through the water. Inside, lamellae — comb-like filters — strain algae and shrimp from the brine.

B. TWELVE VERTEBRAE

The neck holds twelve cervical vertebrae. Humans hold seven. That extra bone math is what permits the famous S-curve and the inverted feeding posture.

C. ANKLE, NOT KNEE

The joint that bends “the wrong way” halfway up the leg is the ankle. The actual knee sits higher, tucked under the body's plumage. Once you see it you cannot un-see it.

D. SALT GLAND

A nasal gland behind the eye filters salt out of the bloodstream and weeps it back out the nostrils. This is why flamingos can drink water that would kill almost anything else.

04 / ONE LEG

NOT FOR WARMTH. not for vanity. FOR EFFICIENCY.

The standing-on-one-leg pose is not a trick of balance. The flamingo's leg joints lock into a passive stay-apparatus — a mechanical brace — so that standing on one leg requires almost no muscular effort at all. It is the most economical way for the bird to rest. Studies have shown that a sedated, dead flamingo can be balanced on one leg. The bird is, in effect, a self-supporting tripod that has misplaced a leg by design.

05 / HABITAT

THE BIRD CHOSE THE WATER
nothing else wanted.

Alkaline lakes. Brine lagoons. Mudflats so caustic that a footprint will burn through leather. Lake Natron in Tanzania — pH 10.5, sometimes 60°C — is one of the most important breeding grounds on earth. The flamingo did not adapt to a hostile environment. It chose the environment nothing else wanted.

06 / FLAMBOYANCE

A GROUP IS CALLED A FLAMBOYANCE.

Tens of thousands of birds synchronizing. Head-flagging, wing-saluting, twist-preening, marching — a choreography that ripples across the lake with no choreographer. Courtship rendered as theater on an industrial scale.

The largest species — pale pink, near-white in low-carotenoid populations. The flamingo of the Camargue and the Mediterranean salt pans.

02

AMERICAN
FLAMINGO

Phoenicopterus ruber

  • RANGECARIBBEAN · GALÁPAGOS
  • HEIGHT120–145 CM
  • STATUSLC · least concern

The poster species. The most saturated reds in the family — almost orange under direct sun. Lives where diets are richest in carotenoids.

03

CHILEAN
FLAMINGO

Phoenicopterus chilensis

  • RANGES. AMERICA
  • HEIGHT110–130 CM
  • STATUSNT · near threatened

Identifiable by grey-blue legs with bright pink joints — as if the legs were assembled from spare parts.

04

LESSER
FLAMINGO

Phoeniconaias minor

  • RANGEEAST AFRICA · INDIA
  • HEIGHT80–90 CM
  • STATUSNT · near threatened

The smallest and most numerous — populations into the millions. Forms the iconic pink crowds that fill Lake Bogoria and Natron.

05

JAMES'S
FLAMINGO

Phoenicoparrus jamesi

  • RANGEHIGH ANDES
  • HEIGHT90–100 CM
  • STATUSNT · near threatened

Lives at 4,000+ metres on freezing salt flats. Was thought extinct for half the 20th century until a remnant population was rediscovered in 1956.

06

ANDEAN
FLAMINGO

Phoenicoparrus andinus

  • RANGEBOLIVIA · CHILE · PERU · ARG

The only flamingo with yellow legs. Rarest of the six. Breeds on the Salar de Atacama — now being mined for the lithium in your phone.

08 / CONSERVATION

THREE OF SIX SPECIES
ARE DISAPPEARING.

The flamingo lives in the world's most fragile water — high-altitude salt flats and shallow alkaline lakes that took millions of years to form and can evaporate in a decade. Their habitats are now being drained for irrigation, scraped for soda ash, and increasingly excavated for lithium for the batteries in cars, laptops and phones.

The Andean salt flats sustain three species at once. They are also the world's largest lithium reserve. The most colorful birds on the planet are vanishing into the supply chain for things that are not pink at all.
